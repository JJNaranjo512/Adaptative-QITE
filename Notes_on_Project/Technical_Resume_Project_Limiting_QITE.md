# Practical Scaling Limitations of QITE on Noisy Quantum Processors

## 1. Executive Research Context and Strategic Importance

In the current Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) era, the transition from theoretical quantum advantage to practical utility is fundamentally obstructed by hardware-induced decoherence and gate errors. Variational Quantum Algorithms (VQAs) are central to our strategy for circumventing the exponential scaling of Hilbert space by utilizing parameterized circuits. Specifically, Quantum Imaginary Time Evolution (QITE) has emerged as a high-priority candidate for ground-state energy estimation and nuclear structure simulations. By applying the Wick rotation ($\tau = it$), QITE maps non-unitary evolution onto gate-based hardware, theoretically providing a path to robust state preparation.

However, a critical gap persists: most literature evaluates QITE under idealized, error-free conditions, obscuring the severe practical risks of variational data propagation. This research identifies a profound "bottleneck" in the hybrid classical-quantum loop. We challenge the prevailing optimism by demonstrating that the sensitivity of the variational principle to hardware noise creates a hard ceiling on algorithmic feasibility. This investigation establishes that hardware-imposed errors do not merely degrade results—they fundamentally corrupt the parameter update trajectory, moving us toward a rigorous mathematical bounding of the "performance floor" for QITE.

## 2. Theoretical Framework: The Variational Propagation Workflow

The strategic mapping of non-unitary imaginary time dynamics onto unitary, gate-based processors is governed by McLachlan’s variational principle. This principle serves as the mathematical conduit through which we evolve a parameterized density matrix $\rho(\theta(\tau))$ to approximate the exact state propagation.

### Technical Workflow Synthesis

The QITE propagation requires the iterative solution of a linear system of equations that dictates the parameter update: $M\dot{\theta} = Y$ (Or $M\dot{\theta} = V$ for real-time evolution). Following the rigorous definitions provided in the research, the matrix and vector elements are calculated as follows:

- **Matrix Elements (****M_{k,q}****):** Defined using the Frobenius norm definition and trace of partial derivatives: $M_{k,q} := \text{Tr} [ (\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta_k})^\dagger \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta_q} ]$.
- **Vector Elements (****Y_k****):** Crucially, to maintain normalization and faithful trajectory tracking, the vector must include the expectation value term: $Y_k := -\text{Tr} [ (\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta_k})^\dagger (\{H, \rho\} - 2\langle H \rangle \rho) ]$.

The "So What?" Layer of this framework is the extreme vulnerability of the $\dot{\theta}$ update. Because the propagation relies on solving a linear system, the inversion of the M matrix represents the single most fragile point in the hybrid algorithm. Any perturbation in the measurement of M or Y is amplified during the classical optimization step, making the linear solver the primary conduit for hardware noise to destabilize the entire quantum trajectory.

- **Derivation-level companion, with code:** [[QITE_Hand_Off/Notes_on_Project/Understanding_Quantum_Imaginary_Time_Evolution_and_its_Variational_Form|Understanding_Quantum_Imaginary_Time_Evolution_and_its_Variational_Form]] (Anglés-Castillo et al., arXiv:2510.02015) re-derives this same $M\dot\theta=Y$-type update (there $M\dot\theta=-V$, mind the sign) from first principles for varQITE, alongside the original (non-variational) QITE algorithm it's compared against, and releases a working, noiseless SciPy/Qiskit implementation of both — a useful noiseless baseline before layering in the noise bounds above.

## 3. Characterization of the Global Markovian Noise Model

To derive universal performance bounds, we must move beyond local noise heuristics and model environmental influence as a global Markovian channel $\mathcal{E}(\rho) := p\mathcal{N}(\rho) + (1-p)\rho$. This approach captures the worst-case scaling inherent in correlated environments.

### Model Specification

- **CPTP Map Conditions:** The noise channel $\mathcal{N}$ is a Completely Positive and Trace Preserving map satisfying the Frobenius norm ($\|\cdot\|_2$) contractivity condition: $\| \mathcal{N}(X) \|_2 \leq \|X\|_2$.
- **Vulnerability Mechanism:** Unlike simple gate-level noise models, this analysis accounts for the increased depth required for ancilla-based measurement circuitry. Differentiation ($\partial\rho/\partial\theta_k$) necessitates controlled-gate operations and controlled-SWAPs, where global noise $\mathcal{E}(\rho)$ exerts its most damaging influence on the integrity of M and Y.
- **Noise Types:** This model spans probabilistic unitary errors, including random Pauli errors and partially depolarizing/dephasing channels. We assume idealized state preparation and measurement to isolate the catastrophic impact of gate-level errors on circuit scaling.

Strategically, the global error model provides a rigorous performance floor. By capturing how noise scales across the entire circuit width and depth, it reveals the stringent gate fidelities required to maintain a valid variational trajectory in high-depth regimes.

## 4. Analytical Error Bounds and Sensitivity Analysis

Effective algorithm design in the NISQ era requires moving beyond numerical trial-and-error toward universal performance bounds. Using perturbation theory, we define how noise probability p and parameter count N bound the relative error in the parameter update.

### Theorem 1 Synthesis (General Noise)

For general probabilistic errors, the upper bound for the relative error \epsilon / \|\dot{\theta}\| is a function of the condition number \text{cond}(M): \frac{\epsilon}{\|\dot{\theta}\|} \leq \text{cond}(M) \left( (1-(1-p)^{2N})(1+\frac{N}{2\|M\|_2}) + (1-(1-p)^N)(1+\frac{\sqrt{N}}{\sqrt{2}\|Y\|_2}) \right) This bound is mathematically valid only within the range of p where second-order error terms are negligible, defined by: 0 \leq p \leq 1- \left( 1- \frac{2N^2\delta}{N + 2\|M\|_2} \right)^{\frac{1}{2N}}

Two caveats established by [[QITE_Hand_Off/Notes_on_Project/Replication_Practical_Limitations_QDP|Replication_Practical_Limitations_QDP]]. First, the last term is written here as \sqrt{N}/(\sqrt{2}\|Y\|_2), which is what Eq. (A37) of the source derives; the paper's Eqs. (17) and (A38) print \sqrt{N}/\sqrt{2\|Y\|_2} instead. Neither reading reproduces Fig. 3(b) — only the \|Y\| \to \infty limit does, and the paper never reports \|Y\| for the 5-parameter ansatz at all. Second, the appendix form of the bound (Eq. A42) carries a denominator 1 - \text{cond}(M)\|\Delta M\|/\|M\| that the main-text Eq. (17) drops; that denominator turns negative above p \approx 4 \times 10^{-4} (N=5), so the appendix bound is invalid across essentially the whole range the paper plots. Use the main-text form.

### Theorem 2 Synthesis (Depolarizing Noise)

Under partially depolarizing noise, the error scales exactly as: \frac{\epsilon}{\|\dot{\theta}\|} = \frac{1-(1-p)^N}{(1-p)^N} This exact expression is Hamiltonian-independent, relying solely on the hardware noise p and the circuit depth/parameter count N.

Reproduced end-to-end from a density-matrix simulation with a global depolarizing channel after every gate, agreeing to 2.8 \times 10^{-7} across p \in [10^{-4}, 0.2] ([[QITE_Hand_Off/Notes_on_Project/Replication_Practical_Limitations_QDP|Replication_Practical_Limitations_QDP]]). One refinement follows from that exercise: Appendix B of the source applies noise only to the derivative line and leaves the \rho inside \{H, \rho\} ideal. On hardware both quantities are read from the same noisy device, and in that setting the relative error is exactly **half** of the expression above, i.e. \dot{\theta}^{err} = \dot{\theta}[1 + (1-(1-p)^N)/(2(1-p)^N)]. The mechanism is that Y_k = -\text{Tr}[\partial_k \rho H] is minus the energy gradient for a normalized state, so the maximally mixed admixture contributes a term collinear with \dot{\theta}. Theorem 2 is therefore a factor of two pessimistic as a model of a hardware QITE step.

### The "So What?" Layer: Condition Number Amplification

The strategic takeaway is the role of the **Condition Number** (\text{cond}(M)). In our benchmarking, we observed that even relatively "small" condition numbers—such as 20.22 for a 9-parameter universal ansatz or 66.7239 for a 5-parameter H_2 ansatz—are sufficient to trigger rapid divergence. When M is ill-conditioned, the linear system M\dot{\theta} = Y becomes fundamentally ill-posed. Even hardware noise as low as 10^{-3} is amplified until the parameter update \dot{\theta} is effectively randomized, leading to total algorithmic failure.

**Status of these two figures.** Both are quoted from Appendix C of [[QITE_Hand_Off/Notes_on_Project/Practical_Limitations_of_Quantum_Data_Propagation_on_Noisy_Quantum_Processors|Practical_Limitations_of_Quantum_Data_Propagation_on_Noisy_Quantum_Processors]] and neither has reproduced independently — see [[QITE_Hand_Off/Notes_on_Project/Replication_Practical_Limitations_QDP|Replication_Practical_Limitations_QDP]]. Rebuilding M from Eqs. (6)–(8) at the published angles yields \text{cond}_F(M) = 863.26 for the 5-parameter ansatz, not 66.7239, and no combination in a 60-row scan over initial state, gate set, wire order and metric normalization lands within 5% of the reported value. For the 9-parameter universal ansatz the situation is structural rather than numerical: a two-qubit *pure* state manifold carries 2d-2 = 6 real dimensions, so a 9 \times 9 metric built on it is necessarily rank-deficient. The computed M has rank 6 with three singular values at the 10^{-17} level, hence \text{cond}(M) = \infty, and the reported 20.22 is recoverable only under a pseudo-inverse cutoff the paper does not state. The construction used for this check is validated against the paper's own closed form for M_{kk} (Eq. B9) to 10^{-10}, so the disagreement is with Appendix C rather than with our implementation.

The qualitative claim of this section is unaffected: ill-conditioning amplifies noise, and the amplification mechanism is confirmed exactly by Theorem 2 (reproduced to 10^{-10} from a noisy simulation). What must be dropped is the use of 20.22 and 66.7239 as *established* condition numbers. The rank deficiency is the load-bearing finding for the adaptive-step programme: if M is singular by construction at the ansätze of interest, then \dot{\theta} is already the output of a regularized solve, and the regularization cutoff is a free parameter that an adaptive time-stepping controller would otherwise be tuning against blindly. The cutoff has to be promoted to an explicit component of the method.

**Extension (re-verified after the project-wide audit): cond(M) is not static even along a single trajectory.** [[QITE_Hand_Off/Notes_on_Project/Adaptive_QITE_Attack_Route_3_Implementation|Adaptive_QITE_Attack_Route_3_Implementation]] answers this section's own open question (item 1 of Section 7 below) directly: running an adaptive Euler QITE controller on the 5-parameter ansatz from a generic (non-Appendix-C) starting angle, \text{cond}_F(M) climbs into the 10^5-10^6 range as the flow itself approaches a near-degenerate point in parameter space — a stiffness event that develops *during* propagation, not a property of any one fixed angle. A fixed-step Euler integrator run through the same trajectory overshoots there and its energy error briefly increases; an adaptive controller that caps \Delta\tau proactively on \text{cond}(M) collapses its step size smoothly and does not overshoot.

The project-wide audit ([[QITE_Hand_Off/Audits/Audit_Project_Wide/Audit_Adaptative_QITE|Audit_Adaptative_QITE]]) found the accept/reject gate this claim originally shared a run with was itself mis-specified (gating on the exact-ITE identity rather than the ansatz's own variational prediction, [[QITE_Hand_Off/Audits/Audit_Project_Wide/M4_Controller_Near_Convergence|M4_Controller_Near_Convergence]]), which left two candidate causes for the \Delta\tau collapse conflated. Re-run with the gate corrected: over the last quarter of the trajectory the \text{cond}(M) cap is the binding constraint on 31 of 43 steps and the energy-band gate never triggers a retry — and the unregularized spectral condition number (not bounded by the pseudoinverse cutoff the way \text{cond}_F is) independently reaches 1.4\times10^6. The stiffness reading survives on firmer evidence than the original write-up had. This confirms empirically that condition-number monitoring (Section 6) needs to run throughout the trajectory, not just be checked at the ansatz's initial angles.

**Corroboration from an unrelated algorithm: the silent gauge choice is structural, not an artifact of our ansätze.** [[QITE_Hand_Off/Notes_on_Project/Beyond_Trotterization_Variational_Product_Formulas_for_Quantum_Simulation|Beyond_Trotterization_Variational_Product_Formulas_for_Quantum_Simulation]] (Assi, Vogl, Kumari & LeBlanc, Phys. Rev. B **113**, 214314) derives *real*-time product formulas from a global action principle, producing the parameter equation of motion $\sum_k g_{jk}\dot{c}_k + iF_j = 0$ with $g_{jk}$ the quantum geometric tensor — the same linear-solve structure as $M\dot\theta = Y$, in real rather than imaginary time. That paper reports no conditioning diagnostic for $g$ anywhere. It is nonetheless singular at the initial time by construction: any ansatz that repeats a generator (their $ABA$ and $ABAB$ orderings) has $T_1(c_1) = \text{Tr}[Ae^{ic_1B}Ae^{-ic_1B}] \to \text{Tr}[A^2]$ as $c_1 \to 0$, which makes two rows of $g$ coincide at $t = 0$. Evaluating their printed Eq. (35) at their own Fig. 2 parameters ($h_x=5$, $h_z=2$) confirms $\text{rank}(g) = 2$ of $3$ at $t=0$, with null vector $\propto (1, 0, -1)$ — the sum $c_0 + c_2$ is determined there, the difference is not — and $\text{cond}(g)$ diverging as $t^{-2}$ on approach ($2.5\times10^5$ at $c_1 = 10^{-3}$, $2.5\times10^{11}$ at $c_1 = 10^{-6}$). Their symmetric choice $c_0 = c_2$ is therefore a null-space gauge fixing, adopted on grounds of symmetry, never named as such, with the integrator started from the singular point.

**Quantified: the degeneracy is a boundary layer, and that distinction is now measurable.** The conditioning table above was initially recorded as prose. It is now pinned as target T6 of [[QITE_Hand_Off/Notes_on_Project/Replication_Beyond_Trotterization|Replication_Beyond_Trotterization]], hard-coded with no tolerance knob and re-asserted by `replicate.py --check`. The replication also supplies what the static table could not: $\text{cond}(g)$ evaluated *along* the trajectory rather than at a single $c_1$. For the Ising model at $N=5$ it falls from $3.4\times10^{12}$ ($BAB$) and $2.1\times10^{16}$ ($ABAB$) at the integration start to $1.36$ and $4.85$ by $t=2$. Correspondingly, the converged error $\mathcal{E}_F(t{=}2)$ shifts by at most $5.12\times10^{-10}$ in relative terms as the start time $t_0$ is varied over $[10^{-8},10^{-3}]$ and the pseudo-inverse cutoff `rcond` over $[10^{-14},10^{-4}]$.

Three distinct regimes have therefore now been observed, and they carry different consequences for step-size control. In the nine-parameter ansatz of Appendix C the metric is rank-deficient *by construction at every point of the manifold*, so the cutoff is a genuine free parameter and the reported condition number is unfalsifiable. In the Route 3 trajectory the stiffness *develops during propagation* as the flow approaches a near-degenerate region, so monitoring must run throughout. In the present real-time case the degeneracy is confined to a *boundary layer at the initialization point* and the answer is provably insensitive to how it is regularized. Only the first two require the cutoff to be promoted to an explicit component of the method; the third is correctly handled by starting from $t_0 > 0$ at the Trotter–Suzuki value rather than by regularizing more aggressively. A controller that cannot distinguish the three will over-regularize the benign case and under-monitor the dangerous one, so classifying the regime should precede choosing the response.

This remains corroboration by analogy rather than a new measurement on $M$: it concerns a real-time unitary ansatz rather than the QITE metric, and the degeneracy is benign — now in the specific, measured sense above — at the two- and three-parameter sizes that paper actually runs. Its value is to generalize the finding above. Rank-deficient geometric tensors carrying unstated regularization are not a peculiarity of the $H_2$ ansätze of Appendix C — the same structure recurs in an independent line of work at single-digit parameter counts. The methodological conclusion therefore holds more broadly than first stated: a published result resting on a geometric-tensor solve should be read as carrying an implicit regularization or gauge choice until its authors state otherwise, and our own work should log that choice explicitly rather than inherit the convention.

## 5. Numerical Benchmarking: The H_2 Minimal Basis Case Study

We benchmarked these theoretical bounds using the H_2 Hamiltonian in a minimal STO-3G basis (Bravyi-Kitaev representation) to evaluate the impact of ansatz complexity on error scaling.

### Data Point Extraction and Scaling

- **Ansatz Comparison:** We compared a 5-parameter simple ansatz against a 9-parameter universal ansatz. The data shows that the error, defined as the relative norm \|\Delta\dot{\theta}\|_2/\|\dot{\theta}\|_2, scales aggressively with N.
- **The Divergence Point:** As visualized in [SOURCE_IMAGE_1], there is a clear "divergence" point where the relative error exceeds 100%, rendering the propagation meaningless. While QITE may appear stable for H_2 (N=6) at very low p, it is projected to fail almost immediately for larger systems like LiH (N=14).
- **The Bottleneck Conjecture:** For current hardware, the threshold for reliable propagation is extremely narrow. As p increases beyond 10^{-4}, the error growth is so rapid that the calculated \dot{\theta} fails to track the ground state. This confirms that without significant post-processing, QITE is practically unscalable on existing NISQ backends.

## 6. Success Roadmap: Requirements for the Future Researcher

A Senior Researcher must approach QITE with a strategic understanding of these noise thresholds. Success depends on moving beyond "black-box" optimization toward a deep awareness of the M matrix's numerical stability.

### Essential Knowledge Base

- **Condition Number Monitoring:** Researchers must track the empirical \text{cond}(M) throughout the trajectory. High sensitivity in the linear solver is a more immediate threat than circuit depth alone.
- **Depth-Error Scaling:** One must master the trade-off between ansatz expressibility and the noise induced by N (or depth d). Every additional parameter exponentially tightens the required gate fidelity 1-p.

### Proposed Mitigation Strategies

- **Error Suppression:** Probabilistic Error Cancellation (PEC) is the most viable path to suppressing error growth in the evolution, though it introduces significant sampling overhead. Zero Noise Extrapolation (ZNE) is likely to fail in this context, as the additional circuit layers required would likely push the system past the divergence points identified in Theorem 1.
- **Classical Post-Processing:** Employing matrix inversion approximations or regularization techniques for the M matrix can help mitigate the ill-posed nature of the linear system, though these are stop-gap measures for truly high-depth simulations.
- **Adaptive step-size control (implemented, corrected after the project-wide audit):** [[QITE_Hand_Off/Notes_on_Project/Adaptive_QITE_Attack_Route_3_Implementation|Adaptive_QITE_Attack_Route_3_Implementation]] builds and runs a concrete controller — proactive \text{cond}(M) cap, McLachlan-residual caution signal, energy-monotonicity/variance-identity accept/reject gate — on top of an explicit, logged pseudoinverse cutoff. [[QITE_Hand_Off/Audits/Audit_Project_Wide/Audit_Adaptative_QITE|An audit]] found and fixed a mis-specified accept/reject gate ([[QITE_Hand_Off/Audits/Audit_Project_Wide/M4_Controller_Near_Convergence|M4_Controller_Near_Convergence]]) and a wrong hardware-noise debias factor ([[QITE_Hand_Off/Audits/Audit_Project_Wide/M1_Depolarizing_Debias_Correction|M1_Depolarizing_Debias_Correction]]) in the original implementation; the numbers below are post-correction. On the 9-parameter (rank-6-of-9) ansatz, a tight cutoff that removes only the manifold's true null space still reaches a lower final energy than a loose cutoff that reports a numerically *lower* \text{cond}(M) but discards real gradient information along with the null space (error 8.9\times10^{-4} vs. 2.1\times10^{-3}, \sim2.4\times) — direct evidence that \text{cond}(M) alone is not a sufficient signal for the "right" regularization choice, and that the cutoff and the step-size controller need to be tuned together, not independently. (The pre-correction numbers overstated this gap by roughly an order of magnitude — the loose-cutoff run was additionally being stalled by the mis-specified gate, not just under-regularized.)
- **Related lever — twirled imaginary-time evolution (TITE):** [[QITE_Hand_Off/Notes_on_Project/Trading_Imaginary_Time_for_Randomness_in_Ground_State_Preparation|Trading_Imaginary_Time_for_Randomness_in_Ground_State_Preparation]] (Arulandu, Martyn & Chuang) addresses a different but adjacent cost driver: the exponential sample/classical cost of ITE implementations in the target imaginary time $\beta$. Appending randomly-timed real-time evolution after ITE quadratically suppresses trace distance to the ground state, allowing $\beta \to \beta/2$ for the same accuracy. Because it is implementation-agnostic, this may compose with the variational QITE update analyzed here without touching the $M\dot\theta = Y$ solve itself — worth evaluating as an orthogonal cost reduction, separate from the condition-number/noise-floor problem above.
- **Related lever — variational product formulas for the real-time sub-block:** [[QITE_Hand_Off/Notes_on_Project/Beyond_Trotterization_Variational_Product_Formulas_for_Quantum_Simulation|Beyond_Trotterization_Variational_Product_Formulas_for_Quantum_Simulation]] (Assi, Vogl, Kumari & LeBlanc, Phys. Rev. B **113**, 214314) attacks circuit depth rather than noise. Instead of fixing product-formula exponents by algebraic order conditions, it derives them from a global action principle applied to the evolution *operator*, yielding Hamiltonian-specific rotation angles; because the variation is over $U(t)$ and not over a state, one solve serves any initial state at fixed $H$. Reported gains against Ruth's fourth-order formula at $N=10$: $\sim 50\%$ fewer $R_x$ and $\sim 33\%$ fewer $R_z$ and CNOT gates per layer (their Table I), at *lower* error ($\approx 1.5\%$ vs. $> 3\%$ at $t=1.0$, their prose on p.8). The gate counts reproduce exactly; the error figures do not. [[QITE_Hand_Off/Notes_on_Project/Replication_Beyond_Trotterization|Replication_Beyond_Trotterization]] obtains $2.12\%$ and $5.2$–$6.9\%$ at that point — matching their own Fig. 4(a) to $1\%$ while missing the prose by $41\%$ and $73$–$129\%$ — so the correct statement of the gain is the *ratio*, $2.46\times$, which survives both readings. Three caveats before treating this as usable here. The construction is entirely noiseless — the NISQ benefit is inferred from gate counts, never simulated. And its cost is displaced into a classical precomputation of traces over a $2^N$-dimensional space, which is tractable in closed form only for the quantum Ising and XXZ chains they study; the MPO and Hadamard-test routes offered for general $H$ are asserted, not benchmarked. And the advantage is confined to the large-step regime: the four-exponential ansatz reduces to Trotter–Suzuki as $t \to 0$ and is therefore only third-order accurate, against fourth order for Ruth, so below a crossover at $t = 0.376$ (at $N=10$, $h_x=h_z=J=1$) the formula it replaces is the more accurate of the two — contrary to the paper's claim of an advantage over the entire interval. A step-size controller that shrinks $\tau$ to suppress Trotter error walks out of the regime where these angles buy anything. Its relevance to this project is indirect but real: depth is the $N$ entering the Theorem 1 bound, so anything buying accuracy per layer relaxes the gate-fidelity requirement. It also composes naturally with TITE above, which needs a real-time propagator and is agnostic to how that propagator is built.

## 7. Top-Level Research Inquiry for Absolute Clarity

To characterize a QITE implementation on a specific hardware backend, the researcher must answer the following:

1. **What is the empirical condition number of the** **M** **matrix across the evolution trajectory?**
2. **Does the hardware gate fidelity** **1-p** **satisfy the bound required by Theorem 1 for the chosen** **N****?**
3. **Can the ansatz be re-optimized to reduce depth** **d** **while maintaining sufficient ground-state overlap?**
4. **Is the noise profile sufficiently depolarizing to utilize the mitigation shortcut suggested by Theorem 2?**
5. **What is the sampling overhead required for PEC to reduce** **\epsilon** **to within acceptable tolerances?**
